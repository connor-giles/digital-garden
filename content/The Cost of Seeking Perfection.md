In my years, I have learned many things about myself, and I could go into large detail about my quirks and nuances, but that is for another time. I want to to instead draw attention to my fear of making mistakes and my desire to *do everything right*. 

Figuring out where this tendency in myself came from is a topic for another day, today we will merely focus on the fact it exists and what that means. 

I fear that I have deprived myself of many learning opportunities due to my fear of failure. I felt as though I *had to get it right* on the first try. Which is obviously not true. However, another thing that was recently brought to my attention that loosely relates to this idea, is that of missed volume of improvement, bare with me on the name I gave it. 

This is simply the idea that over time, if you seek to only do if you can do perfectly, you end up doing much less. There is a story about two professor of art who teach a pottery class, one teacher states that the students will be "graded on the skill of their final pot", while the other class will be "graded on the volume of pots thrown" over the course of the semester. It was found that the professor who encouraged volume over skill, had students who made higher quality pots, simply due to the increased number of attempts, even though there was never an explicit mention of quality in this professor's guidelines. This is because they made pots, failed, learned, and got better. 

So whether its coding, my job, the gym, or even writing these ideas down into an obsidian vault, I fear I have deprived myself of so many learning opportunities over the years. Tasks I never even started out of fear I might not get it right. Theres no better time to make a change than now. Otherwise there will be nothing left to leave behind when [[Long after you are gone|I'm long gone]]


**I will try to fail more... and enjoy it :)**
